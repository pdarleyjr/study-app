# Math Mission: Implementation Rationale (internal)

Scope: closed-book 5th-grade practice (primes, multiples, LCM, LCD, simplifying/equivalent fractions). The child never sees citations. Source checks were done 2026-11-05; "not verified" means the abstract could not be opened directly and the claim rests on indirect records.

## Principles, evidence, and where they live in the game

| # | Principle | Evidence (what was actually found) | Game interaction | Label |
|---|-----------|------------------------------------|------------------|-------|
| 1 | Practice testing (retrieval) beats re-reading | Dunlosky et al. 2013 (Psych Sci in the Public Interest 14(1)) rate practice testing "high utility". Franzoi et al. 2025 (Front Psychol, 10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1632206): 5th graders (age 10-11, N about 25-33, quasi-experiment, **history** texts) learned more from fill-in-gap testing with feedback than re-reading. Ophuis-Cox et al. 2023 (Appl Cogn Psychol 37(6), 10.1002/acp.4141): 48 **2nd graders**, flashcard retrieval beat restudy for multiplication-fact fluency at 5 min and 1 week. | Every item is a closed-book answer attempt before any help; no "read the notes" mode in the Mission. | Directly studied with children (different subjects/ages: history at grade 5, multiplication facts at grade 2; not primes/LCM/LCD) |
| 2 | Interleaving mixed skills | Dunlosky 2013 rate interleaving only "moderate" (limited classroom evidence). Rohrer et al. 2020 (J Educ Psychol 112(1), 10.1037/edu0000367): cluster RCT, **7th graders**, 54 classes, about 4 months; interleaved beat blocked on a test 1 month later (WWC: 62 vs 38; meets standards without reservations). Abstract read only via WWC study page, not the APA page. | Morning Mission uses 11 interleaved items, one per remaining objective, not blocks by topic. | Studied with older students (grade 7) - reasonable transfer to grade 5 |
| 3 | Worked example, then immediate practice | IES practice guide NCER 2007-2004 (Pashler et al.), Rec. 2: interleave worked examples with problems; evidence rated **Moderate**, mostly lab studies, "some classroom experiments". Dunlosky rates self-explanation "moderate". The 2021 WWC guide for struggling elementary students (WWC 2021006) rates systematic, explicit instruction **Strong**, but that is for intervention students and is not a worked-example-specific finding. | On a miss: 20-second worked example, then an easy different-numbers item at once. | Mixed: practice guide and lab evidence on varied ages; reasonable transfer. Exact 20-second length is a design choice, no guarantee |
| 4 | Corrective feedback content | Merrick & Fyfe 2024 (J Exp Child Psychol 241, 105865): children's math; per secondary summaries, feedback that added a verification cue ("right/wrong") alongside the correct answer lowered persistence and strategy variability vs the correct answer alone. Abstract not verified; sample and effect sizes not verified. 2021 WWC guide recommends immediate corrective feedback. | Feedback shows the correct answer plus the reason in brief, in neutral language; no scolding, no score shaming. | Directly studied with children (details not verified); how we apply it is an inference |
| 5 | Retest after a short delay | Retrieval-after-feedback is supported by item 1. Spacing literature: Cepeda et al. 2006 (Psych Bull 132(3)) meta-analysis of verbal recall (adults mostly); best gap grows with retention interval. Pashler 2007 Rec. 1 (Moderate) spaces review by **weeks to months**. Franzoi 2025 found **no significant difference** between 1-day and 4-day gaps. | Missed skill is retested about 3 items later, then in the final 6-item check. | **A few minutes is NOT multi-day spacing.** It is a within-session retrieval retest, not the distributed-practice effect. Design choice, no guarantee |
| 6 | Distributed practice across days | Dunlosky 2013 rate distributed practice "high utility" (broad ages and tasks). | Doing the Mission on several days before the test (parent/child planning; progress records skill history). The game cannot enforce this. | Studied with older students and adults mostly - reasonable transfer; the app's contribution is only making a daily habit short |
| 7 | Easy warm-up recall | General retrieval benefit (item 1); confidence/low-stakes start is a motivational choice. | 4 easy items first. | Design choice, no guarantee |
| 8 | Sleep | AASM consensus (Paruthi et al. 2016, J Clin Sleep Med 12(6):785-786): children 6-12 "should sleep 9 to 12 hours per 24 hours on a regular basis to promote optimal health." Verified on PMC4877308. This is a health recommendation, not a claim that sleep boosts one math score. | Short sessions, a gentle "finish early, rest well" note; no late-night nudges or streak pressure. | Directly about children (health guidance, not a learning-outcome trial) |
| 9 | Emergency 5-min mode | No direct evidence for this format. | 7 mixed items, misses first, brief corrective feedback, no extra teaching. | Design choice, no guarantee |
| 10 | Mini-games and 15-item Test Ready Challenge | No study of these formats. Fresh numbers and a single attempt reduce memorizing answers. | Prime Patrol, Multiple Match, Fraction Forge; Challenge uses generated numbers, one attempt per item. | Design choice, no guarantee |
| 11 | First-try vs corrected tracking; no timers or leaderboards | Honest measurement design; avoiding pressure is a values choice. WWC 2021 recommends timed activities for fluency (moderate) but these are for a different goal; we omit timers on purpose. | Two separate counters: first-try correct, corrected-after-explanation. No clocks, no rankings. | Design choice, no guarantee |

## What we deliberately did NOT do / do not claim

- No guarantee of a test score or grade. Evidence is group-average and mostly from other subjects, ages or lab settings.
- No "subliminal", "brain-hacking" or "learn while asleep" claims.
- No late-night cramming prompts; sleep guidance above is health advice, not a performance promise.
- "Test Ready" means practiced, not proven mastered. Readiness is not proof of mastery.
- Same-day retests are not described as "spaced repetition".
- Dunlosky's "high" ratings do not mean the specific timings in this design (8-12 min, 20-second example, 3-item retest) are tested.
- No unverified citations shown to the child.
