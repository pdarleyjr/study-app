First Americans

Historians believe the first settlers came from Asia. During the Ice Age, people may have walked over Beringia or traveled by boat along the Pacific coast. They spread south from Alaska, then east and south across the continent.

Desert: hot, dry, little rain. Forest: trees, animals, rainfall. Mountains: rocky, colder high up, little flat land. Plains: flat, grassy, good for hunting and farming.

Culture and environment

Climate, physical features, and natural resources shaped homes, clothing, food, tools, and farming. Rivers gave people water, food, and travel routes. Inuit families used warm animal-skin clothing, built snow or ice homes, hunted sea animals and fish, and used every part of the animals.

Three regions

Northwest Coast: Pacific coast from Alaska to California; mild and very rainy; cedar forests, fish, animals, rivers, and ocean; Kwakiutl, Chinook, Tlingit.

California–Intermountain: California coast to the Sierra Nevada and Great Basin; mild near the coast, dry with extreme temperatures in the Basin; shells, redwood, reeds, berries, fish, small game, desert plants; Pomo, Miwok, Shoshone, Paiute.

Southwest: Southwestern U.S. into northern Mexico; hot days, cold nights, very dry; clay, adobe, few trees, desert plants, and dry farming of corn, beans, squash; Hopi, Apache, Ancestral Pueblo.
